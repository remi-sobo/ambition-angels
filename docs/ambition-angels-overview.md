## 1. The one-paragraph version

Ambition Angels is a 501(c)(3) youth development nonprofit that puts real career exposure in teenagers' pockets. Its product, **the Ambition app**, delivers **30-day simulated career internships** — 15 minutes a day, on the phone a teen already owns — across 19 career tracks. It's free for every student, and teens earn real rewards (gift cards) for finishing. The core insight, learned the hard way: the app does the exposure work, but a **trusted adult** has to make it stick. So the 2026 organization is built around equipping that adult — a counselor, mentor, coach, or parent — with a Guide dashboard and conversation prompts tied to whatever the teen is working on right now.

**Tagline:** "Freshman Year to Career." / "Career exposure. In their pocket."

## 3. Origin story

Remi Sobomehin and Demetric Sanders grew up together in Portland, Oregon, both attending Remi's father's community nonprofit as kids. They grew up in low-income, inner-city communities with no roadmap, set their sights on Stanford, and eight years later — with the support of dedicated youth developers — both made it.

After graduating, Remi moved to East Palo Alto and went to work leading in schools and nonprofits, trying to replicate what had worked for him. He kept hitting the same wall: **he could not get teens to show up.**

Meanwhile Demetric joined Facebook and worked on the product team focused on teens. He could see exactly who *was* reaching them at scale: their phones.

The numbers that became the founding thesis:
- Only **11%** of teens attend after-school programming
- More than **95%** own a smartphone
- They spend roughly **8 hours a day** on it

The framing the org uses: *"That is not a crisis to solve. That is the most powerful channel in the history of youth development."* So they built for it.

**Remi's personal note:** born into a family dedicated to social justice and community service; went to Stanford to understand and address the barriers facing Black youth; now homeschools two children with his wife Kendra in East Palo Alto, staying rooted in the community he serves. His quote, used across the site: *"My parents ran youth nonprofits throughout my childhood and instilled in me a dedication to serve communities that have been left behind. All of our youth deserve the highest quality investments, and when that reality comes to fruition, we all benefit."*

**Demetric Sanders** (Tech Partner): first-generation college graduate who overcame childhood adversity; worked on Facebook's teens team.

## 4. The problem being solved

**The framing used publicly:** *"The gap is not ambition. It is access."*

- Teens from low-income communities graduate knowing academic subjects but not how to make their way in a workforce that is changing faster than any curriculum can keep up with.
- Youth not connected to a viable career path by age 25 are more likely to face unemployment, poverty, and housing instability.
- Career uncertainty by **age 16** predicts disconnection from education and work by the mid-20s *(OECD Career Readiness Research, 2021)*.
- Traditional programs never reach them — only 11% attend after-school programming.
- Most teens have been exposed to **fewer than 5 careers**, usually whatever their parents do.
- The systems meant to help are contracting: DEI rollbacks, reduced government investment in youth services, erosion of school counseling.

**The evidence base for the intervention:**
- **40 of 47** longitudinal studies found teens with early career exposure had better adult employment outcomes, with wage gains of **5–10%** not uncommon, even controlling for academic achievement and social background *(OECD Career Readiness Review, 2021)*.
- The 5–10% adult wage boost is tied specifically to early career exposure — even career conversations, job shadowing, and simulated experience showed measurable long-term impact *(OECD, From Classroom to Career, 2025)*.
- **Career conversations with a trusted adult by age 15** are one of the strongest independent predictors of adult employment outcomes *(OECD, 2021)*.
- Corporate-facing version of the claim: a teen with structured career exposure is **more than twice as likely to be employed** than a peer without it.

Framing used on the evidence: *"We did not invent the link between career exposure and economic mobility. We built a program to help deliver it at scale."*

## 5. The model

### 5a. The Ambition app — the core product

Three-step framework used everywhere on the site:

1. **Pick your path** — internships in entrepreneurship, sales, game design, nursing, wealth management, and more. Careers teens may never have considered.
2. **Show up to work** — videos, quizzes, and activities built for the phone screen. 15 minutes a day. No commute, no classroom.
3. **Earn for your effort** — complete an internship and earn gift cards from brands teens actually use. *"Their time has real value. We honor that."*

**Mechanics:**
- Each internship is a **30-day immersion in one career**: roughly 20 videos, 10 quizzes, 10 hands-on activities, designed for 15 minutes a day on a phone.
- Every track has a fictional employer, a real deliverable, and six phases.
- Intended rhythm: **four internships a year, one per quarter** — four real careers tried on by year's end.
- **Free for every student**, always.
- Rewards: partners decide how to recognize their own students (often without money); Ambition keeps a focused reward pot for the highest-need teens.

**The animating principle:** *"Instead of paying adults to develop young people, we reward teens for developing themselves."*

**What a teen gets by day 30:** they know what that career actually feels like — what it pays, whether it fits. Then they try something else. They start seeing careers they didn't know existed, and start believing those careers are for them.

**Skills taught, in two buckets:**
- *Technical:* marketing, sales, digital design, product thinking — what employers are hiring for.
- *21st-century:* communication, problem-solving, empathy, time management. *"These are not soft skills. They are the foundation of every career that lasts."*

### 5b. The key insight — the trusted adult

This is the single most important strategic statement in the current materials:

> "We built Ambition to go directly to teens. And we were right that the phone was the channel. But we learned something critical: the teens who complete internships and actually shift their career thinking are almost always connected to an adult who knows they are doing it. A counselor. A coach. A mentor. A parent. Someone who can say: tell me what you learned today. **That insight is reshaping everything we are building in 2026.**"

So the org stopped asking how to get teens to activate on their own, and started asking: *where are the adults who already care about these teens?*

### 5c. One tool, four settings

There are four answers to that question, and Ambition Angels is in all four:

| # | Setting | The Guide | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **In school** | Advisory teachers and counselors | First full-year integration underway |
| 02 | **In community college** | Program counselors | Pilot in progress (MESA) |
| 03 | **In after-school programs** | Mentors and program leaders | **Proven** — 36 partners |
| 04 | **With parents** | Parents and family | Rolling out now |

Notes on each:
- **School:** advisory periods and career-connected learning blocks — the adult already has the room, roster, and schedule. First full-year in-school integration starts this fall, which is also what's driving the move of the platform to the web so students can use school computers.
- **Community college:** cohort programs whose entire measure of success is helping students finish and land somewhere. Closest setting to what Ambition already does.
- **After-school programs:** mentoring orgs, youth programs, juvenile justice partners. Where the org started and where partners already run it.
- **Parents:** the adult with the most invested and the least support. Same conversation guides, built for the dinner table instead of the classroom.

> "The tool does not change across those years [14 to 24]. What changes is who the guide is, how much guiding a young person still needs, and what their setting makes possible."

**The current strategic experiment:** *"We are not running four experiments and hoping one works. We are running one tool through four doors, and watching which one opens widest."* Each setting has to earn its own evidence. By end of 2026 they intend to know **which setting carries the most weight per adult trained** — and build the next phase around it. If it's high school advisory → every district running an advisory model. If it's community college → MESA alone is 96 campuses. If it's programs → grow through the mentoring networks they're already inside.

### 5d. Two ways teens are reached

1. **Through organizations** — nonprofits, schools, and youth-serving programs. Teens pick an in-person guide they already trust; Ambition equips that adult, and the adult does the talking. Named partners include **Friends of the Children, EOYDC, Live in Peace, Pivotal, The Hidden Genius Project**, and — a significant 2026 development — **juvenile detention centers via Fresh Lifelines for Youth**.
2. **Direct to the adults in their corner** — parents, coaches, mentors who find Ambition on their own. Same app, completely different experience built for the adult.

## 6. The programs and products

### The Guide dashboard / Guide portal
The adult-facing layer. Program Partners get their own login: student progress, completion rates, what each student is working on right now, and **career conversation prompts tied to the specific module a student just finished**. A consumer Guide portal for parents/mentors is in waitlist/pilot (`/for-adults`).

Sample conversation prompts published on the site:
- "After doing the entrepreneurship internship — what surprised you most about running a business?"
- "If money wasn't a factor, what kind of work do you think you'd actually enjoy doing every day?"
- "What's one career you learned about this week that you'd never considered before? What made it interesting?"

### "The Deal"
Before a teen starts, the adult makes an agreement with them: pick a reward, make it real. Examples published:
- *Parents:* complete 5 internships → pizza party of your choice
- *Teachers:* finish 3 internships → one get-out-of-a-test-free card
- *Coaches:* complete the sports business track → new kicks

The reward can be monetary, an experience, or a privilege. *"The point is the agreement."*

### The Ambition Fund
For teens who don't have an adult who can make the deal with them. Low-income teens apply directly through the app, make an agreement with Ambition Angels itself, and earn real rewards as they complete internships. Donor-funded. Also described to funders as the partner-directed reward pot for the highest-need teens.

### Ambition Coaches
Industry professionals matched one-to-one with a teen who's gotten serious about a direction. **Four virtual sessions over four weeks**, focused on turning a goal into a plan. Recruitment, vetting, and infrastructure for the first cohort is a 2026 priority; first pilot in progress. Also offered as a deep-engagement option for corporate partners' employees.

### Program Partner onboarding (self-serve)
Four steps, same-day access: create account → get a unique program code → block **15 minutes, twice a week** → students start learning and earning. No staff training, no contract, cancel anytime. Two entry modes: *Channel Partner* (Ambition presents to the group and students self-start) and *Program Partner* (recommended — built into program time, with the Guide dashboard).

Who it's built for: after-school programs, coaches and athletic programs, faith communities, juvenile justice programs, foster care and group homes, mentorship programs.

The standard objection and the standard answer: *"You're already running a program. You don't need another thing."* → *"You create 15 minutes in your schedule twice a week. Your staff doesn't run it. The app does. You just create the time."*

### The Career Quiz
An AI-powered career matching tool on the public site (homepage + curriculum page) that points a teen toward tracks worth exploring.

### "What Are You Built For" (`/ms`)
A middle-school career game — 30 taps, ~8 minutes, no sign-up and no email — that produces a deterministic RIASEC-based ranking and a deck of real jobs with real pay, drawn from U.S. Department of Labor O\*NET and BLS data. Has a solo mode and a group/classroom mode with room codes. Unlisted (shareable by link only), not in site nav.

### MESA partnership (`/mesa`) — the community-college pilot
A career-readiness partnership with **MESA at Cañada College** (Redwood City), director **Rance Bobo**.

- **50 students** in the pilot year
- **3 internships funded per student**, **$100 gift card** per completed internship, fully funded by Ambition Angels
- **4 coaching sessions** for students ready to go deeper
- Launch window: **mid-September 2026**, after August orientations; year-end review

Three-layer structure, narrowing: **Exposure** (all 50 students, the app) → **Conversations** (the MESA team, the Guide dashboard) → **Connections** (the few ready to go further, an Ambition Coach).

Why it matters strategically: MESA reaches first-generation STEM students across **more than 90 California community colleges** (the update cites 96 campuses). One college now; a model that could travel.

## 8. Impact — the numbers

**Headline stats (used across the site):**

| Number | What it is |
|---|---|
| **3,500+** | Teens reached since launch |
| **87%** | From Title I schools |
| **14%** | Increase in future orientation (specifically Action Orientation) |
| **1,100+** | Hours of career exploration delivered |
| **36+** | School and nonprofit partners |
| **74%** | Start a second internship |
| **1,000+** | Active since public launch, June 2025 |

**The headline outcome, unpacked:** the **Future Orientation Score (FOS)** tracks how teens think about and move toward their futures, measured pre and post every program cycle. **Action Orientation** — whether a teen is actually taking steps toward a career, not just imagining one — is one element within the FOS and showed the **biggest gain of any dimension tracked, +14% across 1,000+ teens**.

> "Teens do not just feel better about the future. They act on it." … "Career exposure alone does not break the cycle of poverty. But teens who can see a path — and start walking it — have a meaningfully better shot."

**The evaluation framework — two areas, four measures:**
- *Future Orientation Score:* **Future Orientation** (can a teen envision a future, do they believe they have choices, do they feel agency) and **Action Orientation** (concrete steps toward a future they can see).
- *Career Exposure:* **Exploration Breadth** (how many fields and roles meaningfully engaged) and **Career Conversation Quality** (are teens talking to adults about their futures).

One question behind all of it: *does this program put teens on a different economic trajectory?*

**Student voices used publicly:**

### "Why now" — the case for urgency

Two arguments run in parallel:

**1. AI is restructuring the job market.** The careers that survive are built on what makes us human. The six skills Ambition threads through every track, conversation guide, and coaching session:

> Curiosity · Creativity · Critical Thinking · Communication · Relationship Building · Business Mindset

**2. The support systems are contracting while the money sits unspent.** DEI rollbacks, reduced government investment in youth services, erosion of school counseling. At the same time: *"there is significant public funding available right now for career-connected learning, and much of it is going unspent. Not because schools do not want it, but because there is no straightforward way to deliver real career exposure to a few hundred students at once. That is the exact gap we built for. The need is growing and the money is already appropriated. What is missing is the delivery."*

### Sustainability — who pays when the grant ends

The stated answer, already in motion: **corporate partnership, not logo placement.** Companies bring their own people in to *author the career content*. Employees sit for a one-hour session or record a ten-minute clip about the work they actually do, and Ambition produces it into a 30-day internship thousands of teens move through. The company gets employee engagement, reportable outcomes, and a real relationship with the next generation of workers in their field. Ambition gets content built by people doing the job, and a revenue line that isn't a grant.

Built at two levels: national companies whose customers are already students and families, and community-level companies who want their name attached to young people in their own market. Partners are vetted carefully — *"A sponsor's name on our platform is a signal to every partner and family who sees it."*

Rationale: foundation funding runs one to three years; corporate relationships, when both sides get something, run much longer. **Earned revenue comes after** — *"We are not going to ask a school to pay for something we have not yet proven inside a school. Prove it this year, price it next."*

## 10. The corporate partnership offer

Pitch: *"Show the next generation where work is going."*

**How employees show up (deliberately low-lift, called micro-volunteering):**
- **Career learning sessions** — ~20 employees, one guided hour on Zoom, talking through one career: technical skills, day to day, honest parts. Recorded and produced into a sponsored 30-day internship. Best for groups.

- **Partner count.** The homepage, `/impact`, and the "four settings" card all say **36** school and nonprofit partners. The Q2 2026 update's stat bar says **20 partners running it now, with 50 more in the pipeline**. Most likely reading: 36 total partner relationships, 20 actively running. Confirm before publishing either.
- **MESA campus count.** The `/mesa` page says "more than 90 California community colleges"; the investor update says **96 campuses**. Both are in circulation.
- **Sponsorable tracks.** The `/companies` page lists some tracks (Dental Hygiene, Real Estate, Engineering) that are not among the 19 currently live — that list is illustrative of what a sponsor could build, not a catalog of what exists.
- The headline four-up stat bar (3,500+ / 87% / 14% / 1,100+) is now driven from a single source in the codebase, so those four are consistent everywhere.
