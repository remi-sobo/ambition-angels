# 01. Surgical Technologist
**Field:** Health · **The invisible one** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/surgical-technologists.htm

### THE DAY *(private. 60 seconds. Nobody else sees this.)*

> I get to work at 6:15 and change clothes before anyone else is in the room. My first job is the count. Every tool, every sponge, every needle, laid out in the order they will be needed. I count them out loud while somebody listens. At 7:40 the person we are helping comes in, already asleep. My table is ready before anyone asks. For the next three hours I stand two feet from the person doing the work and I put things in their hand before they ask for them, because if I am fast, they never have to look up. At 11:05 something starts bleeding that was not supposed to. Nobody panics. I already have the clamp in my hand. At 12:30 we count everything again. The number has to match. It always has to match.

### THE CLUES *(read out loud, one at a time)*

1. **The problem I solve.** Some work is too fast and too important for one person to do alone. Somebody has to stay a step ahead of it.
2. **What I do all day.** I put the right thing in someone's hand at the exact second they need it, before they ask.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Calm hands. A memory for order. Standing still and focused for six hours.
4. **Who is counting on me.** The person lying on the table, and the person working on them who cannot look away.
5. **Where I work.** Healthcare. Hospitals, surgery centers, trauma units.
6. **How you get here.** A 12 to 24 month program at a community college. No four-year degree. You can be working at 20.
7. **What it pays.** Half of us make more than $62,830. The top ten percent make over $90,700. The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** If the count at the end does not match the count at the start, nobody leaves the room. Not the doctor. Not the patient. My count is the last word.

### THE ANSWER
**Surgical Technologist**

# 02. Registered Nurse
**Field:** Health · **The anchor** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm

### THE DAY

> I come in at 6:45 for the handoff. The person leaving tells me about every patient on my floor in about eleven minutes, and I write fast, because whatever she forgets is now my problem. Room 412 is scared and has not slept. Room 409 is not telling me the truth about his pain, and I can tell, because his jaw is tight. At 9:20 I catch something. A number on a screen moved a little bit in the wrong direction and nobody else saw it. I make a call. By 10, a doctor is standing where I told her to stand. At 2:15 I sit down with a family and explain something nobody wants to hear. I do not rush it. That conversation is the job too.

### THE CLUES

1. **The problem I solve.** People are at their most scared and their most confused, and somebody has to be in the room with them the whole time.
2. **What I do all day.** I watch a person closely enough to catch the thing that is going wrong before it goes all the way wrong.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Noticing small changes. Explaining hard things simply. Not panicking.
4. **Who is counting on me.** The patient, their family, and the doctor who is only in the room for eight minutes.
5. **Where I work.** Healthcare. Hospitals, clinics, schools, homes. Nights, weekends, holidays.
6. **How you get here.** A two-year or four-year degree, then a license exam.
7. **What it pays.** Half of us make more than $93,600. The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** The doctor sees the patient for a few minutes a day. I am there for twelve hours. When something changes, I am the one who finds it.

### THE ANSWER
**Registered Nurse**

# 03. UX Researcher
**Field:** Tech · **The invisible one** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/web-developers.htm (Web and Digital Interface Designers)

### THE DAY

> At 9:30 I sit down with a stranger and hand her a phone. I ask her to buy a pair of shoes on it. Then I shut up. That is the hard part. She taps the wrong thing. She taps it again. She says "wait, where did it go," and starts scrolling back up. I say nothing. I am writing. She gives up at four minutes and says "I'd probably just call somebody." That sentence is worth more than everything my company built last month. At 2:00 I do it again with a different stranger, and he gets stuck in the exact same place. Now it is not a fluke, it is a fact. At 4:30 I stand in front of the people who built the thing and I tell them, kindly, that eleven out of twelve people could not use it.

### THE CLUES

1. **The problem I solve.** The people who build a thing can never see it the way a stranger sees it. Somebody has to go find out the truth.
2. **What I do all day.** I watch regular people try to use something, and I write down every place they get confused.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Staying quiet when you want to help. Asking a question without hinting at the answer. Telling smart people bad news.
4. **Who is counting on me.** The team that spent a year building something, and every person who is about to try to use it.
5. **Where I work.** Technology. App companies, banks, hospitals, car companies. Anyone with an app.
6. **How you get here.** A four-year degree in psychology, design, or computer science. Some people get here from a certificate and a portfolio.
7. **What it pays.** Half of us make more than $98,090. The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** My whole job is to prove that the people I work for are wrong. And they pay me to do it.

### THE ANSWER
**UX Researcher**

# 04. Software Developer
**Field:** Tech · **The anchor** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm

### THE DAY

> Nothing works when I get in, and that is normal. Somebody changed something last night and now a screen that has worked for two years is broken. I spend from 9:15 to 11:00 hunting for one wrong character. One. I find it, I fix it, and nobody will ever know it happened, which is fine, because that is the whole idea. After lunch I get to build. Someone asked for a thing that does not exist yet, and I get to decide how it should work. I try it. It fails. I try it again. It fails differently, which means I am getting closer. At 4:40 it runs clean for the first time and I sit there and look at it. Tomorrow, a hundred thousand people will use it and not one of them will think about me.

### THE CLUES

1. **The problem I solve.** Somebody has an idea for something that does not exist yet, and somebody has to actually make it exist.
2. **What I do all day.** I write instructions so exact that a machine can follow them without ever guessing what I meant.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Breaking a big problem into small ones. Being wrong a hundred times without quitting. Explaining your work to people who will read it years later.
4. **Who is counting on me.** Everybody who uses the thing. They will only notice me when I mess up.
5. **Where I work.** Technology, but also banks, hospitals, games, cars, farms. Everybody needs this now.
6. **How you get here.** Usually a four-year degree. Some people get here from a bootcamp and a portfolio of things they built.
7. **What it pays.** Half of us make more than $133,080. The top ten percent make over $211,450. The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** Most of my day is not building. Most of my day is figuring out why something that used to work stopped working.

### THE ANSWER
**Software Developer**

# 05. Supply Chain Analyst
**Field:** Business · **The invisible one** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/logisticians.htm (Logisticians)
**⚠️ VERIFY PAY BEFORE SHIP.** Pull the current median directly from the OOH logisticians page. My working figure is roughly $82,000, and I am not shipping a number I have not read off the source.

### THE DAY

> At 8:05 I find out a ship is eleven days late. That is my whole morning now. Somewhere on the ocean there is a metal box with 40,000 of something in it, and it is not going to get where it is going, and three thousand people are expecting it. I open a spreadsheet. I find another box, in another place, and I do math on whether a truck can beat the ship. It can, but it costs four times as much. I call somebody in another country at 11 and we argue about it politely. At 2:30 I make the call. Truck. At 4:00 I go looking for why the ship was late in the first place, because if I fix the ship, I never have this morning again.

### THE CLUES

1. **The problem I solve.** Things have to be in the right place at the right time, and the world is very large and very late.
2. **What I do all day.** I figure out how a thing gets from where it is made to where somebody needs it, and what to do when that goes wrong.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Being good with numbers. Staying calm when something breaks. Making a decision without all the information.
4. **Who is counting on me.** Everybody who is waiting for the thing and has no idea I exist.
5. **Where I work.** Business. Stores, factories, hospitals, the military, anybody who ships anything.
6. **How you get here.** A four-year degree in business or a related field.
7. **What it pays.** [VERIFY: BLS OOH, Logisticians, May 2024 median.] The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** Everything in your house got there because somebody like me made about forty decisions you will never hear about.

### THE ANSWER
**Supply Chain Analyst** *(BLS calls this a Logistician)*

# 06. Marketing Manager
**Field:** Business · **The anchor** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/management/advertising-promotions-and-marketing-managers.htm

### THE DAY

> At 9:00 I look at a number that went down and I have to explain why. Not guess. Explain. Last month we spent a lot of money telling people about something, and fewer people bought it than the month before, and my job is to know the reason. At 10:30 I sit with the people who make the ads and I tell them the one we all loved is not working, which nobody wants to hear at 10:30. At 1:00 I talk to three actual customers and one of them says something so simple and so obvious that I feel dumb. She did not know the product could do the thing she needs. That is not her fault. That is mine. At 3:45 I throw out the plan and start a better one.

### THE CLUES

1. **The problem I solve.** A company can build something great and still fail, because nobody ever found out it existed.
2. **What I do all day.** I figure out who needs a thing, what they actually care about, and how to say it so they hear it.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Reading numbers. Understanding people. Killing your own good idea when it stops working.
4. **Who is counting on me.** Every person at the company, because if nobody buys it, nobody gets paid.
5. **Where I work.** Business. Every industry, every company, anywhere something is sold.
6. **How you get here.** A four-year degree, then a few years doing the work before you run it.
7. **What it pays.** Half of us make more than $161,030. The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** The best part of my job is being told my idea was wrong, because now I know something true.

### THE ANSWER
**Marketing Manager**

# 07. Industrial Designer
**Field:** Creative · **The invisible one** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/industrial-designers.htm

### THE DAY

> I have a shoe on my desk that does not exist. I made it out of foam on Tuesday and it is wrong. It looks right and it feels wrong, and I have been holding it for twenty minutes trying to figure out why. At 10:15 I hand it to four other people and just watch their hands. All four of them hold it the same way, and it is not the way I designed it to be held. That is the answer. At noon I draw it again. At 2:00 I get on a call with a factory in another country and they tell me the curve I love will cost eleven cents more per shoe, and eleven cents times two million shoes is a real conversation. At 5:00 I make version nineteen.

### THE CLUES

1. **The problem I solve.** Everything a person touches was shaped by somebody. If it was shaped badly, they will hate it and never know why.
2. **What I do all day.** I decide what an object looks like, how it feels in your hand, and how it gets built without costing too much.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Drawing. Watching how people actually use things. Being fine with version nineteen.
4. **Who is counting on me.** The factory that has to build it, and the millions of people who will pick it up.
5. **Where I work.** Design and manufacturing. Shoes, phones, cars, chairs, toothbrushes, video game controllers.
6. **How you get here.** A four-year degree in design, and a portfolio that shows what you have made.
7. **What it pays.** Half of us make more than $79,450. The top ten percent make over $134,840. The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** Look around the room right now. Every single object you can see is somebody's job. Somebody decided that.

### THE ANSWER
**Industrial Designer**

# 08. Graphic Designer
**Field:** Creative · **The anchor** · BLS: bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/graphic-designers.htm

### THE DAY

> At 9:00 somebody tells me to "make it pop" and will not tell me what that means. This is most of my mornings. So I ask questions until I find out what they actually want, which is usually not what they said. At 11:00 I make three versions of the same thing and I already know which one is right, but I show all three, because people need to choose. At 1:30 they pick the wrong one. At 2:00 I explain, without being rude, why the one they picked will not work at the size it needs to be. At 3:15 they change their mind. At 4:45 it is done, and it will be on a wall, or a shelf, or a phone, and about nine hundred thousand people will look at it for one second each and never think about it again. That is the job. One second, done right.

### THE CLUES

1. **The problem I solve.** A person will decide in about one second whether to trust something, and that decision is almost entirely about how it looks.
2. **What I do all day.** I turn an idea into something you can see, and I make it clear before I make it pretty.
3. **Three skills that matter.** Listening for what somebody actually meant. Knowing when to stop. Taking criticism without taking it personally.
4. **Who is counting on me.** The person paying for it, and the stranger who has one second to understand it.
5. **Where I work.** Creative. Studios, agencies, magazines, tech companies, or your own kitchen table as your own boss.
6. **How you get here.** Usually a four-year degree. Always a portfolio. The portfolio matters more than the degree.
7. **What it pays.** Half of us make more than $61,300. The top ten percent make over $103,030. The typical American worker makes $49,500.
8. **The thing nobody knows.** Every logo, every sign, every album cover, every app icon you have ever seen. A person made that. On purpose. It did not just appear.

### THE ANSWER
**Graphic Designer**
